Honestly I used to think diaries were for people in old movies, the kind who write by candlelight and hide the key in a drawer. Then our coach made the whole team keep a training log, and I get it now. A diary is a record you can check when your memory lies to you. I was sure I had been running faster in March, but the log said otherwise, and the log was right. People keep diaries for the same reason shops keep accounts: without numbers and dates you just make up a story that flatters you. There is also the part nobody admits, which is that writing things down calms you down. After a bad game I would write three sentences about what went wrong and then I could actually sleep. It turns a loop in your head into a list on paper. I think that is why so many famous people kept journals too, not because they expected anyone to read them, but because it helped them decide what to do next. My diary is mostly about practice and exams. It is not deep. But it is mine, and it is honest in a way my social media never is.
