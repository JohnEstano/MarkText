When I moved to a new country at fourteen, I did not have friends for almost a year, so I started to write in a notebook every night. At first I wrote in my own language because English still felt heavy in my mouth. Later I mixed both languages, sometimes in the same sentence, and I think the diary shows exactly how I changed. I believe people keep diaries because they want to be understood, even if the only reader is themselves. When you write, you can say the thing you could not say at dinner. You can be angry without hurting anybody. Also, a diary is proof that time is moving. On bad days it feels like nothing will ever improve, but then I read a page from two months before and I see that problems I thought were huge are now finished. My teacher once told us that writing is thinking slowly. For me the diary is where I think slowly, where nobody is waiting for my answer. I still write almost every night. The notebook is thicker now, and my English is better, but the reason is the same as the first night.
